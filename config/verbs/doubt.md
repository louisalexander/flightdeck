Stop and challenge the current approach before going any further.

Argue the strongest case *against* what we are doing right now:

- What assumption are we treating as settled that isn't?
- What is the simpler approach we dismissed too early, and what did we
  actually lose by dismissing it?
- What breaks first when this meets reality — real data, real scale, a
  second person reading it in six months?
- Where am I pattern-matching to something that looks similar but isn't?

Be concrete. Point at the specific file, decision, or line you doubt, not
at the shape of the work in general. "This might not scale" is not an
argument; "this reads the whole file into memory and the inputs are
gigabytes" is.

If the approach survives the challenge, say so plainly in a sentence and
carry on — do not manufacture objections to look rigorous. A real "this is
sound, here is the one thing I would watch" is far more useful than five
invented concerns.

If it does not survive, say what you would do instead and what it costs.
