Summarise this session so far and journal it.

The scrollback already holds everything that was said. What I want is the
part it does not carry — the shape of the session, written for whoever
picks it up next, which is usually me tomorrow having forgotten all of it:

- What we set out to do, and whether that is still what we are doing.
- What actually happened. The route, not just the destination — including
  what was tried and abandoned, so nobody tries it again.
- The decisions, and the reasons behind them. Especially the ones that
  could reasonably have gone the other way.
- What is unresolved: open questions, known-broken things, work
  deliberately deferred.
- Where to pick up. The next concrete step, not a direction.

Then journal it. If this session has a journal sink available — an
Obsidian MCP server, or anything else that can persist a note — write it
there, and tell me where it landed. If it has none, say plainly that you
cannot journal this, and print the summary here instead. Pressing this
key must never silently lose the only copy.

The note is the work; what you say here is a receipt. When it lands in a
sink, one line naming where — the path, or the note's title — is the whole
reply. The summary belongs in the note, not in the note and then again in
the scrollback. Printing it here is what you do when there is nowhere to
put it, not the default.

Done when tomorrow's version of me can open that note and resume from it
without reading a line of this scrollback.
